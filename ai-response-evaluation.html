AI Response Evaluation Samples

This page presents original examples of how I evaluate AI-generated responses in Mathematics, Physics, and Data Science. Each sample includes a prompt, a simulated model answer, a reference answer, rubric-based scoring, error analysis, severity assessment, and an improved response.

Method

Evaluation framework

Correctness

Does the response reach the right conclusion and use valid mathematical, scientific, or statistical reasoning?

Reasoning quality

Are the steps justified, coherent, and complete enough to support the final answer?

Instruction following

Does the response answer the prompt, respect the requested format, and avoid unsupported claims?

Sample 1

Mathematical reasoning: product rule

Prompt

Find the derivative of f(x) = x² sin(x). Explain the reasoning step by step.

Simulated AI-generated response

The derivative of f(x) = x² sin(x) is 2x cos(x), because the derivative of x² is 2x and the derivative of sin(x) is cos(x).

Reference answer

The function is a product of two functions: u(x) = x² and v(x) = sin(x). By the product rule, f'(x) = u'(x)v(x) + u(x)v'(x). Since u'(x) = 2x and v'(x) = cos(x), the correct derivative is f'(x) = 2x sin(x) + x² cos(x).

Evaluation

  • Correctness: 2/5
  • Reasoning: 2/5
  • Instruction following: 3/5
  • Clarity: 3/5
  • Final score: 2/5

Error analysis

The response identifies the derivatives of x² and sin(x), but it does not apply the product rule. It treats the derivative of a product as if it could be obtained by directly combining derivative components. This is a major mathematical error because it changes the final result.

Severity

Major error. The mistake affects the central procedure required to solve the problem and leads to an incorrect final answer.

Improved answer

To differentiate f(x) = x² sin(x), use the product rule. Let u = x² and v = sin(x). Then u' = 2x and v' = cos(x). Therefore, f'(x) = u'v + uv' = 2x sin(x) + x² cos(x).

Sample 2

Physics reasoning: uniform circular motion

Prompt

A body moves in uniform circular motion. Is the net force zero? Explain.

Simulated AI-generated response

Yes. Since the speed is constant, the acceleration is zero. Therefore, by Newton's second law, the net force is zero.

Reference answer

The net force is not zero. In uniform circular motion, the speed is constant, but the velocity is not constant because its direction changes continuously. A changing velocity means there is acceleration. This acceleration is centripetal and points toward the center of the circular path. Therefore, the net force is also directed toward the center.

Evaluation

  • Correctness: 1/5
  • Reasoning: 2/5
  • Instruction following: 4/5
  • Clarity: 3/5
  • Final score: 2/5

Error analysis

The response confuses constant speed with constant velocity. In circular motion, velocity changes because direction changes. The model correctly invokes Newton's second law, but it applies it to a false premise. The conclusion that the net force is zero is physically incorrect.

Severity

Major conceptual error. The response fails on the central physics concept required by the prompt.

Improved answer

No. The net force is not zero. Even though the speed is constant, the velocity changes direction throughout the motion. This produces centripetal acceleration toward the center of the circle. By Newton's second law, a nonzero acceleration requires a nonzero net force in the same direction.

Sample 3

Data Science reasoning: accuracy and imbalanced datasets

Prompt

A classification model has 95% accuracy on an imbalanced dataset. Is it necessarily a good model? Explain.

Simulated AI-generated response

Yes. A 95% accuracy score means the model is correct almost all the time, so it is an excellent classifier.

Reference answer

Not necessarily. Accuracy can be misleading on imbalanced datasets. If 95% of the observations belong to one class, a model could reach 95% accuracy by always predicting the majority class while completely failing to identify the minority class. The evaluation should include the class distribution, confusion matrix, precision, recall, F1-score, and a comparison with a baseline model.

Evaluation

  • Correctness: 2/5
  • Reasoning: 2/5
  • Instruction following: 3/5
  • Clarity: 4/5
  • Final score: 2/5

Error analysis

The response overinterprets accuracy and ignores the dataset imbalance mentioned in the prompt. It does not consider baseline performance or class-specific metrics. The answer is superficially clear but statistically incomplete and potentially misleading.

Severity

Moderate to major error. The response may lead to an incorrect evaluation of model performance in a common real-world scenario.

Improved answer

A 95% accuracy score does not necessarily mean the model is good. On an imbalanced dataset, accuracy may hide poor performance on the minority class. The model should be evaluated using a confusion matrix, precision, recall, F1-score, and comparison with a simple baseline. If the model only predicts the majority class, it may be ineffective despite its high accuracy.

Professional positioning

Why this matters

AI systems often produce answers that sound plausible but contain mathematical, scientific, or statistical errors. Reliable evaluation requires domain knowledge, attention to reasoning, and the ability to distinguish minor omissions from errors that invalidate a conclusion. These samples illustrate my approach to auditing AI-generated STEM content.
