Sample 1

Projectile motion

Problem

A projectile is launched from ground level with initial speed v₀ = 20 m/s at an angle of 35° above the horizontal. Neglect air resistance and use g = 9.81 m/s². Estimate the time of flight, maximum height, and horizontal range.

Model assumptions

The projectile is treated as a point mass. Air resistance is neglected. Gravity is constant and acts vertically downward. Horizontal and vertical motions are modeled independently.

Equations

vₓ = v₀ cos(θ), vᵧ = v₀ sin(θ). The time of flight is T = 2vᵧ/g. The maximum height is H = vᵧ²/(2g). The horizontal range is R = vₓT.

Python-style validation

import math

v0 = 20
angle = math.radians(35)
g = 9.81

vx = v0 * math.cos(angle)
vy = v0 * math.sin(angle)
T = 2 * vy / g
H = vy**2 / (2 * g)
R = vx * T

print(T, H, R)

Expected interpretation

The calculation gives approximately T ≈ 2.34 s, H ≈ 6.71 m, and R ≈ 38.3 m. The result is consistent with the standard parabolic trajectory model: increasing the vertical component increases time and height, while the horizontal component controls range.

Sample 2

Exponential decay model

Problem

A quantity decreases by 18% each hour. If the initial value is 120 units, estimate the amount remaining after 6 hours. Interpret the model and its limitations.

Model assumptions

The rate of proportional decrease is constant over time. The process is discrete by hour, so the quantity is multiplied by 0.82 after each one-hour interval.

Equation

Q(t) = Q₀(1 - r)^t, where Q₀ = 120, r = 0.18, and t is measured in hours. For t = 6, Q(6) = 120(0.82)^6.

Python-style validation

Q0 = 120
r = 0.18
t = 6

Q = Q0 * (1 - r)**t
print(Q)

Expected interpretation

The model gives approximately 36.5 units after 6 hours. This does not mean every real process will follow the same curve; the model depends on the assumption that the proportional rate remains constant throughout the interval.

Sample 3

Linear regression and residual reasoning

Problem

A small dataset suggests a positive relationship between study time and exam score. Fit a simple linear regression model and explain why a high correlation should not be interpreted automatically as causation.

Model assumptions

The relationship is approximated as linear over the observed range. The model summarizes association, not causal structure. Other variables, such as prior knowledge, attendance, socioeconomic context, or test difficulty, may also affect performance.

Python-style workflow

import numpy as np
from sklearn.linear_model import LinearRegression

hours = np.array([1, 2, 3, 4, 5]).reshape(-1, 1)
scores = np.array([52, 58, 65, 71, 78])

model = LinearRegression()
model.fit(hours, scores)

slope = model.coef_[0]
intercept = model.intercept_
predicted = model.predict(hours)
residuals = scores - predicted

print(slope, intercept, residuals)

Expected interpretation

The slope estimates the average increase in predicted score per additional hour of study within the observed range. Residuals show how far each observation is from the fitted line. Even if the fitted relationship is strong, regression alone does not prove that study time caused the score increase.

Why this matters for AI evaluation

AI-generated explanations often confuse statistical association with causal inference. A rigorous evaluator must check whether the model distinguishes prediction, correlation, and causation before accepting the conclusion.
