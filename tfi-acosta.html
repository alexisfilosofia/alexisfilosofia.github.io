From historical school records to reproducible analytical evidence.

My current Master's Final Integrative Project develops a reproducible workflow for transforming historical school enrollment archives into structured, validated, analyzable, and communicable data products.

Project orientation

Not only an educational-data project

The project is broader than a conventional dashboard or exploratory analysis. It asks how historical institutional records can be converted into a research-grade data workflow while preserving traceability, uncertainty, privacy, and the connection between each analytical claim and its documentary source.

Documentary workflow

Inventory, source identification, transcription, validation, normalization, and preservation of uncertainty in historical school records.

Data-science workflow

Structured datasets, data dictionaries, quality checks, reproducible scripts, notebooks, tests, outputs, and versioned analytical decisions.

Public communication

Aggregate tables, maps, dashboards, methodological notes, privacy-aware publication, and clear explanation for technical and non-technical readers.

Public pilot

What is already visible

The public GitHub materials present a sanitized and partial methodological pilot. They do not publish raw student-level records, names, addresses, or sensitive coordinates. Their function is to demonstrate feasibility, analytical judgment, and responsible public documentation.

Why it matters professionally

A transferable technical profile

Although the TFI uses historical school archives as its case, the competencies are transferable: messy data ingestion, validation, quality assurance, reproducibility, analytical reporting, privacy-aware publication, dashboarding, and rigorous technical communication. These same capacities are relevant to AI evaluation, data analysis, education, research support, technical QA, and applied analytics roles.

Messy data

Working from imperfect, heterogeneous, historically situated, and partially uncertain source material.

Evidence discipline

Separating observation, inference, normalization, analytical category, and final claim.

Communication

Turning technical and documentary complexity into useful artifacts for different audiences.

Scope

What the public version does not expose

The public repositories are intentionally conservative. They avoid publishing raw archival row-level datasets, student names, family names, raw addresses, or exact point-level coordinates tied to individual records. Public outputs are aggregate, sanitized, synthetic, methodological, or portfolio-oriented.
